## Design Context

### Users

Employees, finance staff, accounts-payable teams, and security/analyst users reviewing a currently opened Outlook email before clicking a link, sharing credentials, changing supplier details, or authorising a payment. Their job is to understand concrete evidence quickly and follow the organisation's verification process without needing security expertise.

### Brand Personality

Forensic, calm, and exact. FraudLens should feel like a trustworthy instrument that explains its findings, never a sensational consumer antivirus product or an opaque AI oracle.

### Aesthetic Direction

A light, compact forensic report designed for the narrow Outlook task pane. Use cool-tinted paper and ink, hairline rules, condensed evidence labels, and monospace only for machine data such as domains and rule codes. Severity uses restrained steel, brass, and brick tones. Avoid floating-card stacks, gradients, glass effects, excessive rounding, and alarmist language.

### Design Principles

1. Evidence before score: concrete comparisons and recommended actions lead; the numeric score is supporting detail.
2. Verified and inferred are visibly distinct through labels and provenance, never colour alone.
3. Progressive disclosure: employees see the decision and next action first; analysts can expand the calculation trace.
4. Missing Outlook capabilities are stated honestly as unavailable or unknown, never converted into suspicious evidence.
5. The task pane remains legible and operable from narrow classic-Outlook widths through wider new-Outlook panes, with accessible focus, contrast, and reduced-motion behaviour.
